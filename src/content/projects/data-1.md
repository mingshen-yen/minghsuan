Ground motion with strong velocity pulses can cause significant damage to structures at certain periods. Understanding pulse periods and velocity amplitudes is critical for earthquake structural engineering, yet the physical factors governing how pulse periods scale with magnitude remain poorly understood.

#### ***Scope & Methods***

1. Focus on moderate but damaging earthquakes (Mw 6-7)
2. Pulse identification using the Shahi & Baker (2014) method, accounting for potential static-offset effects
3. Numerical simulations using the frequency-wavenumber algorithm

#### ***Key Findings***

1. Within-event variability of pulse properties is large
2. Identified pulses are predominantly associated with strike-slip earthquakes
3. Asperity location and size strongly influence pulse periods and amplitudes at nearby stations
4. Fault dip also plays a role in pulse variability

#### ***Core Conclusion***

Beyond earthquake magnitude, asperity characteristics are a critical factor controlling the occurrence and properties of pulses generated by the forward directivity effect. The study quantifies and discusses both within- and between-event variabilities of pulse properties at short distances.
